# level0b doubts

- Spelling choices fixed file-wide (pick one if merging with other files): kitaab, almari, jhaadu, pocha, moze, chabi, chaadar, botal, paani, naariyal, mehendi, rokda, mazdoor, sindoor.
- Near-synonym pairs kept as separate words in different groups: chalni (sieve) vs no "channi"; rumala (gurudwara cloth) vs rumaal; chalak (driver) vs chalaak (clever); chimta vs chimti.
- Possible overlap with share (a): "Chai banane ke liye", "Masale ka dabba", "Lohri ki raat", "Train mein bikta hai" contain food words.
- Regional / less universal for level 0: "Pongal ki pehchaan", "Durga pooja", "Shastriya nritya", "Baaki Hindi mahine" (kwar, aghan, poos), "Chhah rituein", "Purane sikke" (dhela, damdi).
- Slang groups may be too informal: "Ladke ke liye boli" (launda), "Pyaar se bewakoof", "Mast ke saathi" (jhakaas, dhaasu).
- Synonym-type groups ("Subah ke naam", "Raat ke naam", "Sansaar ke naam" incl. "loka") lean Shuddh Hindi, not everyday Hinglish.
- "Gali cricket ke niyam" (one tip, deewar out, gali out) and "Sabziwale se moladmol" (dhaniya free) are playful; tile meanings depend on local usage.
- "Board pe khelte hain" includes "business" (generic Indian name of trading board game) - check not treated as brand.
- Family label debatable: many money/office groups tagged money_work; verb groups tagged language.
- Heavy reuse across groups is intentional red-herring material (e.g. diya, gubbara, topi, jhula, chhotu, ghanti).
- "Ramleela"/"Mahabharat" character groups use proper nouns (Ram, Sita, Arjun...) - may overlap religion_myth groups from other writers.
